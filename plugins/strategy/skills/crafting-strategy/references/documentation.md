# Documentation

Guidance for Phase 5.

## Reading order for the final document

1. **What we're doing** (the plan, the approach -- lead with the decision)
2. **How we're doing it** (actions, timeline, owners, launch plan)
3. **Why we believe this is right** (opportunity, landscape, data, hypothesis)
4. **What's out of scope** (what we're not doing and why)
5. **Open questions** (what we don't know yet, what would change our mind)

A reader who trusts you can stop after section 1. A reader who wants to interrogate the reasoning keeps going. Both get what they need.

## Integrating existing work

If there are related proposals or prior documents, compare against them. Call out where the strategy aligns, where it diverges, and why. This builds on the work others have done rather than ignoring it.

## Writing guidelines

- Be specific. "We never compromise on quality" is a bumper sticker, not strategy.
- Show your work. Document rationale so others can adapt when context shifts.
- Include at least one thing you're saying NO to.
- Name the uncomfortable truths in the landscape.
- Keep it to 1-3 pages. If it's longer, it's probably a vision document, not a strategy.
- **Write in the strategist's voice, not the framework's voice.** Use the taste skill and apply rules with the "Strategy Documents" adaptation. If the document reads like it was generated by a template, rewrite it. Use plain language, direct statements, and the words the strategist actually used in conversation.
- **Match process weight to company size.** Before finalizing, scan the doc with a company-size filter. Would a company of this headcount actually run this process, or are we writing the process a bigger company would have? AI brainstorming partners often translate judgment-based criteria into formal approval flows even when explicitly told not to. The human writing the strategy is the last filter against process inflation.
